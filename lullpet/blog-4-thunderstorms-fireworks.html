Thunderstorms & fireworks: a calm-down checklist

6 min read Vet-reviewed Updated 2026

For millions of pets, a thunderstorm or fireworks night is genuine terror — trembling, hiding, panting, sometimes hurting themselves trying to escape. The difference between a bad night and a manageable one is almost always preparation. Here's the checklist.

Why loud nights are so hard

It isn't only the volume. Storms bring shifts in barometric pressure, static electricity, and low-frequency rumble that pets sense before we do. Fireworks add unpredictable flashes and bursts. To an animal, it can feel like the environment itself has turned hostile.

You can't explain the noise to your pet. You can build them a place it can't reach.

Before the night — prepare

  • Check the forecast — and the local fireworks calendar around holidays
  • Exercise early — a good walk hours before; a tired body settles faster
  • Create a safe room — interior, windowless if possible, with their bed
  • Set up sound cover — white noise, a fan, or calm music ready to go
  • Plug in a pheromone diffuser — ideally 24 hours ahead so it's working
  • Feed before it starts — anxious pets often refuse food mid-event

During the event — support

  • Stay calm yourself — pets read your energy; act like it's an ordinary night
  • Don't punish the fear — and don't force them out of hiding
  • Comfort is fine — the old "you'll reinforce fear" myth is outdated; calm reassurance helps
  • Close curtains — blocking flashes reduces the trigger
  • Offer the den — their calming bed in the safe room gives somewhere to retreat
  • Try a snug vest — gentle, even pressure soothes many dogs
Vet-reviewed

For severe noise phobia — self-injury, panic that lasts hours — talk to your vet well before the season. Short-term anti-anxiety medication, used responsibly, can be a humane part of the plan.

After the night

Once it's quiet, return to normal calmly. A short, gentle walk the next morning helps reset their sense of safety. Each well-managed event makes the next one a little easier.

The long game

Between seasons, some owners work on gradual sound desensitization — playing storm or firework sounds very quietly during happy activities, raising the volume over weeks. Done patiently, it can meaningfully reduce the fear over time.

Calming essentials for storm season

A den-style bed, a drug-free pheromone diffuser, and a snug anxiety vest — the calm-down kit, ready before the next flash.
