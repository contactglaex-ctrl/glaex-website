Why your cat ignores their water bowl

5 min read Vet-reviewed Updated 2026

If your cat walks past their water bowl all day, then drinks from the tap, a glass, or a puddle in the shower — they're not being difficult. They're being a cat. And understanding why can protect their long-term health.

The instinct behind picky drinking

Cats descend from desert-dwelling wildcats. In the wild, still water that sits near food can stagnate and harbor bacteria — so cats evolved to favor moving water and to keep water sources away from their meals.

Your cat's "fussiness" is that ancient instinct, intact. A still bowl beside their food dish breaks two of their oldest rules at once.

Cats aren't refusing water. They're refusing water that feels unsafe to drink.

Why it matters

Cats have a naturally low thirst drive and are prone to chronic dehydration. Over time, that contributes to urinary crystals, kidney strain, and lower energy. Hydration isn't a minor detail — for cats, it's central to lifelong health.

Sudden changes in drinking — much more or much less — can signal kidney, thyroid, or diabetic conditions. If your cat's habits shift noticeably, see your veterinarian.

How to get your cat drinking

1. Give them moving water

A pet fountain keeps water circulating, oxygenated, and fresh. For most cats, this single change makes the biggest difference — moving water simply reads as "safe" to drink.

2. Separate water from food

Place water bowls and fountains away from the food dish, and away from the litter box. Multiple stations around the home help, too.

3. Choose the right material

Many cats dislike plastic — it can retain odors and develop tiny scratches that harbor bacteria. Ceramic and stainless steel stay cleaner and are gentler on sensitive chins.

4. Keep it impeccably clean

Rinse daily, deep-clean weekly. Cats are far more sensitive to film and odor than we are.

5. Add moisture through food

Wet food meaningfully increases water intake. Even mixing a little water into meals helps.
