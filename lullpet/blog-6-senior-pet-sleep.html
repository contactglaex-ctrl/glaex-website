Senior Pet Care

Helping an older pet sleep more comfortably

6 min read Vet-reviewed Updated 2026

As pets age, sleep changes. An older dog or cat may circle longer before settling, wake more often, or seem stiff getting up. Good rest becomes harder — and more important. A few thoughtful changes can give your senior pet their comfort back.

Why senior sleep changes

Aging brings stiffer joints, thinner natural padding, reduced circulation, and often a little more anxiety as senses dull. Cognitive changes can disrupt the sleep-wake cycle, too. The result: a pet who wants to rest deeply but struggles to.

Senior pets don't need less comfort. They need more of it, designed with care.

Set up a senior-friendly rest space

1. Support the joints

A supportive bed that doesn't collapse under their weight reduces pressure on aging hips and elbows. A raised rim doubles as a gentle headrest and a sense of security.

2. Keep them warm

Older pets feel cold more easily, and warmth eases stiff joints. A self-warming bed that reflects their own body heat — without electricity — is ideal and safe for unsupervised rest.

3. Make access easy

Place the bed away from drafts and cold floors, on the level where your pet spends most of their day. Avoid making them climb stairs to reach their main resting spot.

4. Protect the routine

Consistent meal, walk, and rest times help regulate a senior pet's internal clock — especially valuable if cognitive changes have begun.

5. Soften the environment

Night lights help pets with fading vision. White noise softens startling sounds. A pheromone diffuser can ease the low-grade anxiety that often grows with age.

Vet-reviewed

Restlessness, pacing at night, or difficulty rising can signal pain or cognitive decline. Senior pets benefit from twice-yearly veterinary checkups — sleep changes are worth mentioning.

Signs your senior pet is resting well

  • Settling within a minute or two, without long circling
  • Longer stretches of unbroken sleep
  • Rising more easily and with less stiffness
  • Calmer, brighter energy during waking hours

Comfort in the later years is one of the simplest gifts you can give. The right bed, warmth, and routine can turn restless nights back into restful ones.
