5 signs of stress in cats most owners miss

6 min read Vet-reviewed Updated 2026

Cats are masters of disguise. In the wild, showing weakness invites predators — so a stressed cat hides it by instinct. That instinct hasn't left your living room. Here are the five signals most owners overlook, and what they're really telling you.

Why cats hide their stress

Unlike dogs, who tend to broadcast distress, cats internalize it. A cat under chronic stress often looks "fine" until the signs become physical. Learning to read the quiet signals early is one of the kindest things you can do for them.

A calm cat shows it. A stressed cat hides it. Your job is to notice the hiding.

The 5 signs to watch for

1. Over-grooming or bald patches

A stressed cat may groom compulsively to self-soothe — often on the belly or legs. If you notice thinning fur or bald spots with healthy skin underneath, stress is a likely cause.

2. Changes in litter box habits

Going outside the box, straining, or sudden frequency changes are rarely "behavioral spite." They are often stress — or a medical issue that stress worsens. Always rule out a vet cause first.

3. Hiding more than usual

Every cat enjoys a quiet nook. But a cat who disappears for most of the day, skips meals, or avoids the family is telling you the environment feels unsafe.

4. Reduced appetite or eating too fast

Stress disrupts appetite in both directions. A cat who suddenly grazes less — or gulps food anxiously — may be struggling. Slow feeders can help anxious eaters settle.

5. Subtle body language

Watch for a tucked tail, flattened ears, dilated pupils in calm light, or a tense crouch. These micro-signals appear long before a hiss or swat.

Vet-reviewed

Many stress signs overlap with medical conditions. If you notice litter box changes, appetite loss, or over-grooming, see your veterinarian first to rule out illness.

Common triggers

  • Change: a move, new furniture, a new pet or person
  • Conflict: tension in multi-cat households
  • Noise: construction, storms, fireworks
  • Boredom: too little enrichment or vertical space
  • Routine breaks: irregular feeding or your travel

How to help a stressed cat

Give them safe vertical space, predictable routines, and enrichment that satisfies their hunting instinct. Pheromone diffusers — a synthetic copy of the calming facial pheromone cats use to mark safe territory — can lower ambient tension in days, drug-free.
